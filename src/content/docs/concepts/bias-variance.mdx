This page gives the math behind [overfitting and underfitting](/concepts/overfitting-underfitting/).

For squared error, the expected prediction error at a point decomposes into three terms:

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[(y - \hat{f}(x))^2\right] = \text{Bias}[\hat{f}(x)]^2 + \text{Var}[\hat{f}(x)] + \sigma^2
$$

- **Bias** is the error from wrong assumptions. A line fitted to a curve has high bias, which is underfitting.
- **Variance** is sensitivity to the particular training sample. A very flexible model has high variance, which is overfitting.
- **Noise** $\sigma^2$ cannot be removed by any model.

## See it in the sandboxes

| Control | Lower bias, higher variance | Higher bias, lower variance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Polynomial degree](/algorithms/polynomial-regression/) | High degree | Low degree |
| [k in KNN](/algorithms/knn/) | Small $k$ | Large $k$ |
| [Tree depth](/algorithms/decision-tree/) | Deep | Shallow |
| [SVM penalty C](/algorithms/svm/) | Large $C$ | Small $C$ |

## What to do about it

Measure on data the model has not seen, using a held-out set or cross-validation. Training error alone always favors the most flexible model. Regularization, ensembles and more data are the main ways to reduce variance.
