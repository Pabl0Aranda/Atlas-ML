## Intuition & Overview

A flexible [polynomial](/algorithms/polynomial-regression/) can bend wildly to chase noise, using huge coefficients that cancel out. Ridge <Term id="regression">regression</Term> charges a price for large coefficients, which trades a little bias for much lower [variance](/concepts/bias-variance/).

## Mathematical Foundation

The objective adds a squared penalty on every coefficient except the bias:

$$
\min_w \; \frac{1}{2n} \lVert y - Xw \rVert^2 + \frac{\lambda}{2} \lVert w \rVert_2^2
$$

It has a closed-form solution, which stays well defined even when <Term id="feature">features</Term> are correlated:

$$
w = (X^\top X + n\lambda I)^{-1} X^\top y
$$

From a Bayesian view, ridge is the most probable $w$ under a Gaussian prior centered on zero. Coefficients shrink smoothly, but they do not reach exactly zero.

## Step-by-Step Breakdown

This site uses coordinate descent, so ridge, [lasso](/algorithms/lasso-regression/) and [elastic net](/algorithms/elastic-net/) share one implementation:

1. Build polynomial features of $x$.
2. Solve each coefficient exactly, holding the others fixed.
3. Repeat passes until the coefficients stop changing.

## Strengths, Limitations & Real-world Trade-offs

**Strengths**

- It stabilizes models with correlated features, or more features than samples.
- It is cheap, and usually a safe default over plain least squares.

**Limitations**

- It keeps every feature, so it does not simplify the model.
- Features must be scaled, or the penalty treats them unequally.
- $\lambda$ needs [tuning](/validation/hyperparameter-tuning/).
