## Intuition & Overview

[K-means](/algorithms/k-means/) makes hard decisions and assumes round <Term id="cluster">clusters</Term>. A Gaussian mixture assumes the data came from several overlapping Gaussian clouds, each with its own shape, size and weight. Each point gets a probability of belonging to each cloud, rather than one label.

## Mathematical Foundation

The density is a weighted sum of $K$ Gaussians:

$$
p(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \pi_k \, \mathcal{N}(x \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)
$$

Expectation-maximization (EM) alternates two steps. The **E-step** computes responsibilities, the probability that component $k$ produced point $i$:

$$
r_{ik} = \frac{\pi_k \, \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k)}{\sum_j \pi_j \, \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_j, \Sigma_j)}
$$

The **M-step** refits each component to its weighted points, with $N_k = \sum_i r_{ik}$:

$$
\pi_k = \frac{N_k}{n}, \qquad \mu_k = \frac{1}{N_k} \sum_i r_{ik} x_i, \qquad \Sigma_k = \frac{1}{N_k} \sum_i r_{ik} (x_i - \mu_k)(x_i - \mu_k)^\top
$$

Each EM iteration cannot decrease the likelihood, though it can stop at a local maximum.

## Step-by-Step Breakdown

1. Initialize the means, here with k-means++, and start with round covariances.
2. E-step: compute responsibilities.
3. M-step: update weights, means and covariances.
4. Stop when the log-likelihood barely changes.

## Strengths, Limitations & Real-world Trade-offs

**Strengths**

- Clusters can be elongated, tilted and overlapping.
- Soft assignments express uncertainty.
- It is a full density model, useful for anomaly detection and sampling.

**Limitations**

- You must choose $K$, although BIC gives a principled guide.
- It is sensitive to initialization, so run several restarts.
- A component can collapse onto a few points, which is why covariances need a small floor.
- Curved clusters still need several Gaussians.
