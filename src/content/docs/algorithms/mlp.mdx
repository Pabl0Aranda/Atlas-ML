## Intuition & Overview

One [perceptron](/algorithms/perceptron/) draws one line. Stack layers of smooth neurons and each layer bends the space for the next, until a problem that was not linearly separable becomes separable. Backpropagation works out how much each weight contributed to the error.

## Mathematical Foundation

Layer $l$ computes an affine map followed by a nonlinearity. Here hidden layers use $\tanh$ and the output uses a sigmoid $\sigma$:

$$
a^{(l)} = \tanh\left(W^{(l)} a^{(l-1)} + b^{(l)}\right), \qquad \hat{y} = \sigma\left(W^{(L)} a^{(L-1)} + b^{(L)}\right)
$$

Training minimizes binary cross-entropy:

$$
L = -\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i} \left[ y_i \log \hat{y}_i + (1 - y_i) \log (1 - \hat{y}_i) \right]
$$

The chain rule propagates an error signal $\delta$ backward. The sigmoid and cross-entropy pair simplifies the output signal to $\delta^{(L)} = \hat{y} - y$, and for hidden layers:

$$
\delta^{(l)} = \left( W^{(l+1)\top} \delta^{(l+1)} \right) \odot \left( 1 - (a^{(l)})^2 \right),
\qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(l)}} = \delta^{(l)} a^{(l-1)\top}
$$

## Step-by-Step Breakdown

1. Initialize weights randomly, scaled by layer width.
2. **Forward pass:** compute every layer's activations.
3. **Backward pass:** propagate $\delta$ from output to input and accumulate <Term id="gradient">gradients</Term>.
4. Update all weights with [gradient descent](/concepts/gradient-descent/).
5. Repeat for many <Term id="epoch">epochs</Term>.

## Strengths, Limitations & Real-world Trade-offs

**Strengths**

- Universal approximators: with enough units they can fit nearly any continuous function.
- They learn their own <Term id="feature">features</Term> and scale with data and compute.

**Limitations**

- The loss is non-convex, so results depend on initialization and tuning.
- They need more data and care than classical models, and they often lose to gradient boosting on small tabular datasets.
- Their internals are hard to interpret.
